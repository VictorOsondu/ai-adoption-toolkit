# Sources and Verification Notes

Last reviewed: 2026-07-24.

This toolkit is practitioner-built. It is intended for practical adoption planning, internal reflection, and lightweight programme support. It is not a validated audit, certification scheme, legal opinion, compliance guarantee, or psychometric instrument.

The GitHub AI Adoption Playbook by GitHub is used and adapted with changes under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Attribution does not imply GitHub endorsement of this toolkit.

## Status of the Toolkit

The tools are generalised from AI Tutorium programme-building work with small businesses, professional audiences, and ministry teams. They are designed to help a team ask better questions before, during, and after AI rollout.

They should be treated as working aids. Teams using AI in regulated, high-risk, employment, education, health, legal, financial, or public-sector contexts should adapt them with appropriate legal, data-protection, HR, safeguarding, procurement, security, and professional review.

## Evidence Boundaries

- GitHub's playbook is a first-party account of GitHub's approach, not independent evidence that every practice will work in every organisation.
- Toolkit adaptations use the underlying operating patterns rather than GitHub-specific mandates, terminology, products, or workforce policies.
- The readiness assessment is a rapid internal scan, not an independent audit.
- Scores are directional. They should prompt action and discussion, not create a public maturity rating.
- Survey results depend on who answers, how safe people feel, and whether leadership acts on feedback.
- Incident logs should support learning and risk reduction, not blame.
- Any claim that a process satisfies a law, regulation, contract, standard, or professional rule should be reviewed by qualified advisers.
