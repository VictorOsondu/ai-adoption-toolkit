# AI Onboarding Checklist

Use this for new staff, contractors, volunteers, or others who will use organisational AI tools. Adapt it to the person's role and risk exposure.

## Direction

- [ ] We explained why the organisation uses AI and what outcomes matter.
- [ ] We made clear that AI is unsuitable for some tasks and that raising concerns is expected.
- [ ] We identified the person's likely role-specific uses rather than giving only generic examples.

## Tools and Access

- [ ] Approved tools, account types, and allowed uses were shown.
- [ ] Personal or consumer accounts were distinguished from approved work accounts.
- [ ] Unnecessary access through plugins, connected apps, memory, sharing, voice, screen, browser, or agents is disabled.
- [ ] The person knows how to request a tool or new use case.

## Data and Output Rules

- [ ] Data classifications and prohibited inputs were explained with relevant examples.
- [ ] The person knows when redaction or synthetic examples are required.
- [ ] Human-review and source-verification expectations are clear.
- [ ] Customer-facing, people-impacting, regulated, or high-risk uses have named limits.
- [ ] Disclosure or record-keeping requirements for the role were covered.

## Practice

- [ ] The person completed one low-risk workflow using approved information.
- [ ] The person checked and corrected an AI output.
- [ ] The person saw an example of a plausible but wrong or unsafe output.
- [ ] The person can explain when they would stop and ask for help.

## Support and Evidence

- [ ] The policy and approved-tool register are easy to find.
- [ ] The relevant champion or community is known.
- [ ] The question, incident, and near-miss routes are known.
- [ ] Completion was recorded where the organisation requires AI-literacy evidence.
- [ ] A refresher or review date was set.
