# Champion Cascade Plan

A lightweight way to move from a pilot group to wider adoption without depending on one central trainer.

Last reviewed: 2026-07-24. See [sources and verification notes](../sources.md).

## 2. Champion Profile

Choose people who:

- use AI responsibly in real work
- can explain things plainly
- are trusted by peers
- know when to escalate
- respect policy and data boundaries

Avoid choosing only the most technical people.

Invite volunteers openly where practical. Motivation and peer trust matter, but manager support and protected time keep the role from becoming unpaid extra work.

## 5. Champion Tasks

- demonstrate one or two approved workflows
- answer basic questions
- collect friction and concerns
- point people to policy and approved tools
- escalate incidents, uncertainty, or sensitive cases
- turn repeated questions into reusable guidance
- share useful failures and limitations as well as wins

Start with a small action: share one use case, answer one question, or help one colleague test a safe workflow.

## 7. Evidence

Review:

- teams with an active champion
- questions answered or escalated
- champion-led demonstrations or sessions
- approved workflows documented and reused
- blockers or risks surfaced early
- specific stories showing useful impact

Membership and message volume alone do not show that the network is working.

## 8. Stop Conditions

Pause the cascade if:

- incidents are not being reported
- staff are unclear about data boundaries
- champions are becoming informal IT, HR, legal, or compliance owners
- AI use is spreading faster than review and support
